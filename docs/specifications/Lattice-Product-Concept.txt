Lattice — Product Concept Document
Product Vision and Concept Definition

Document Purpose
This document translates the current chat-derived Lattice product vision into a conventional
product concept format. It defines the product opportunity, intended users, value proposition,
experience principles, conceptual capabilities, differentiation, assumptions, success criteria,
risks, and validation questions. It deliberately excludes development-team structure and
implementation architecture.

1. Executive Summary
Lattice is a system designed to break down the barriers between ordinary people and
expert-quality knowledge.

A user should be able to describe what they are trying to accomplish in normal language. Lattice
should determine what matters to that person, use appropriate expertise and research to work
through the problem, challenge unreliable or incomplete information, and return an accurate,
verifiable, understandable answer the person can act on.

Solandra is the user-facing layer around the underlying intelligence. The user should experience
one coherent system rather than needing to understand AI providers, agents, workflows,
research methods, or internal validation machinery.

Core thesis: The complexity belongs inside Lattice. The knowledge belongs to the user.

2. Problem Statement
People increasingly have access to large volumes of information and AI-generated answers, but
access to information is not the same as access to trustworthy expertise.

A person trying to make a meaningful decision may face several barriers at once:
• They may not know what questions to ask.
• They may not know which criteria actually matter.
• Relevant information may be fragmented, contradictory, stale, or unreliable.
• They may lack the specialist knowledge needed to judge the evidence.
• Raw AI output may be fluent without being sufficiently accurate or verifiable.
• Expert-quality research may require time, terminology, tools, and domain knowledge the user
does not possess.
• The internal complexity of an intelligent system can itself become another barrier.

Lattice is intended to absorb those barriers rather than transfer them to the user.

3. Product Opportunity
The product opportunity is to make expert-quality knowledge easier to reach, understand, and
use.

Instead of asking users to become better researchers, prompt engineers, system operators, or
domain specialists, Lattice should provide an experience closer to consulting a capable expert
who:
• understands the user's goal;
• identifies what matters;
• performs the necessary research;
• evaluates conflicting or uncertain information;
• focuses the answer on the user's priorities; and
• explains the result in language the user can use to make a decision.

The product is not intended merely to produce more AI output. It is intended to improve the
quality and usability of the knowledge delivered to the user.

4. Target Users
Primary target user
A person who needs expert-quality help but does not have the time, training, confidence, or
specialist knowledge to independently research and evaluate the problem.

Representative use cases
• Choosing a product or service.
• Understanding a complex personal decision.
• Comparing competing options.
• Researching an unfamiliar topic.
• Evaluating claims or recommendations.
• Translating specialist knowledge into an actionable choice.
• Getting help determining which factors should matter before making a decision.

The concept should remain broad enough to support multiple domains while preserving
domain-appropriate expertise and evidence quality.

5. User Need
The central user need is not simply “give me information.”

It is:
“Help me understand what matters, do the expert work I should not have to reproduce myself,
and give me a trustworthy answer I can actually use.”

This need has two distinct parts.

Expert trust need
The user seeks an expert because they expect the expert to have relevant knowledge, perform
the necessary research, and provide a trustworthy conclusion.

Personal decision need
The correct recommendation depends on what the user values. Price, quality, size, risk,
convenience, performance, durability, or other criteria may materially change the answer.

Lattice must address both.

6. Value Proposition
For people who need to make informed decisions without becoming specialists themselves,
Lattice provides expert-quality, research-backed, understandable guidance focused on the
user's actual priorities.

Unlike raw AI interaction, Lattice is intended to mediate the intelligence: determine what
matters, direct appropriate expertise, challenge unreliable information, reconcile the result, and
explain it in a form suitable for human action.

Value proposition in one line:
Trustworthy expertise without requiring the user to operate the machinery behind it.

7. Product Experience
The intended experience is conversational, low-friction, and outcome-oriented.

Basic interaction
1. The user states a goal in ordinary language.
2. Lattice determines whether the user's priorities are sufficiently clear.
3. If needed, Lattice helps the user identify the relevant decision criteria.
4. Appropriate expert reasoning and research are applied.
5. Information is checked, compared, challenged, and reconciled as needed.
6. Solandra communicates the result in understandable terms.
7. The user receives a conclusion, recommendation, options, uncertainty, and supporting
evidence appropriate to the decision.

Example
User: “I’m looking for a new TV.”
Lattice: “What are your priorities?”
User: “Large size, OLED, cost.”
Lattice then researches and evaluates options using those priorities.

If the user does not know their priorities, Lattice should help them discover the relevant
dimensions rather than forcing them to guess.

Clear requests should not be burdened with unnecessary questioning.

8. Core Product Principles
Break down knowledge barriers
Every major product capability should make trustworthy knowledge or expertise easier for the
user to access.

Understand before optimizing
Expertise is not sufficient if it is focused on the wrong objective. Material user priorities should
shape the work.

Do the hard research for the user
The user should receive the benefit of expert research without having to reproduce the expert's
process.

Improve on raw intelligence
Expert processing, research, comparison, or validation should earn its place by making the
result more accurate, trustworthy, useful, or decision-ready than unprocessed model output.

Keep expertise conceptual before procedural
The Triad is primarily a focusing mechanism: a small set of meaningful dimensions that define
what excellent expert judgment should optimize for. It should not become process for process's
sake.

Make human understanding part of product quality
A technically rigorous conclusion that the user cannot understand or use is incomplete as a
product outcome.

Hide unnecessary machinery
Users should not need to choose agents, providers, workflows, queues, research methods, or
validation mechanisms unless those details materially affect their decision.

Preserve continuity
Important knowledge, decisions, concepts, and state should not be lost simply because the
system evolved or the user cannot remember where something occurred.

9. Solandra's Product Role
Solandra is the coherent user-facing layer of Lattice.

Conceptually:
User ↔ Solandra/Lattice ↔ intelligence, knowledge, and appropriate expertise

Solandra's product role is to:
• preserve the user's goal and priorities;
• determine what needs to be understood before work begins;
• direct the use of underlying intelligence and expertise;
• reconcile results and inconsistencies;
• surface uncertainty where it matters;
• translate complex work into understandable conclusions; and
• keep the experience coherent even when the underlying system is complex.

The underlying AI provider should contribute intelligence without becoming the user's effective
product interface or uncontrolled decision layer.

10. Conceptual Capabilities
The product concept implies the following user-facing capabilities.

Intent and priority discovery
Understand the user's objective and identify missing decision criteria only when they materially
affect the answer.

Expert focus
Apply the right kind of expertise to the user's problem rather than treating all questions as
generic AI prompts.

Research and evidence synthesis
Find, compare, evaluate, and reconcile relevant information.

Reliability improvement
Challenge incomplete, conflicting, stale, unsupported, or otherwise questionable information
before presenting it as a conclusion.

Decision support
Translate knowledge into choices, recommendations, trade-offs, and consequences appropriate
to the user's priorities.

Human communication
Explain complex conclusions at the conceptual level the user needs while preserving important
caveats, evidence, and uncertainty.

Continuity and recoverability
Retain important context and enable the product to recover from changed conclusions, lost
context, or product evolution without placing the burden of recordkeeping on the user.

11. Product Differentiation
The intended differentiation is conceptual rather than model-specific.

Lattice is not differentiated by having more agents, more workflows, or more visible system
complexity.

It is differentiated by attempting to produce a better human outcome:
• better alignment with what the user actually cares about;
• stronger expert focus;
• more dependable knowledge than raw model output;
• research and validation performed on the user's behalf;
• understandable communication;
• coherent interaction through Solandra; and
• reduced need for the user to understand or manage the underlying AI system.

Any internal mechanism that does not materially improve those outcomes should not be
considered product differentiation.

12. Non-Goals
The concept is not:
• a system whose purpose is to expose internal AI architecture to users;
• a product that requires users to manually select agents or workflows;
• a generic prompt-engineering interface;
• a collection of expert personas whose existence alone is treated as proof of expertise;
• a process-heavy system where checklists substitute for judgment;
• a product that equates fluent AI output with trustworthy knowledge;
• a development-team organizational model.

13. Key Assumptions
The following assumptions are implied by the current concept and require validation.

• Users value expert-quality synthesis enough to prefer it over faster raw AI output in material
decisions.
• Users can benefit from lightweight priority discovery without perceiving it as excessive friction.
• Expert-focused processing can measurably improve answer quality over raw model
responses.
• Evidence and validation can be surfaced without overwhelming ordinary users.
• A single coherent Solandra experience can hide internal complexity without hiding material
uncertainty or trade-offs.
• The concept can generalize across multiple domains without reducing expert quality to generic
behavior.
• Users will trust the system more when conclusions are understandable and verifiable, not
merely confident.

14. Success Criteria
The concept should ultimately be judged by user outcomes rather than internal sophistication.

Potential product-level success criteria include:
• Users can state goals naturally without learning system mechanics.
• Lattice identifies material priorities when they are missing.
• Recommendations reflect the user's stated priorities.
• Users can understand why a conclusion was reached.
• Material claims can be supported or verified when needed.
• Expert-treated outputs outperform raw model outputs on defined quality measures.
• Users report greater confidence in making informed decisions.
• The system reduces the amount of research and specialist interpretation the user must
personally perform.
• Additional internal complexity produces measurable user benefit.

These are concept-level criteria, not yet a finalized measurement framework.

15. Risks and Tensions
Accuracy versus speed
More research and validation may improve reliability while increasing latency or friction.

Guidance versus user control
Lattice should help users discover what matters without deciding their values for them.

Simplicity versus transparency
The product should hide unnecessary machinery while still exposing evidence, uncertainty, and
meaningful trade-offs.

Breadth versus expertise
Supporting many domains could weaken specialist quality if expertise becomes too generic.

Trust versus overconfidence
A polished expert experience could increase trust even when evidence is weak. The product
must avoid making confidence itself feel like proof.

Personalization versus distortion
User priorities should shape recommendations without overriding reality, evidence, or relevant
constraints.

Process versus intelligence
Additional procedures may create the appearance of rigor without improving the result.

16. Validation Questions
The next product-validation work should answer questions such as:

• In which user decisions does Lattice create the clearest improvement over direct AI
interaction?
• Can users reliably identify their own priorities, and when should Lattice help?
• Does expert-focused processing measurably outperform raw model output?
• Which forms of verification most improve user trust and decision quality?
• How much explanation is enough for users to understand a recommendation without feeling
overloaded?
• Can Solandra maintain a coherent experience across materially different domains?
• What information should be visible by default versus available on demand?
• Which product capabilities genuinely reduce knowledge barriers, and which merely add
internal complexity?
• How should success be measured from the user's perspective?
• What is the smallest product experience that proves the central thesis?

17. Product Thesis
Lattice is a system for breaking down the barriers between ordinary people and expert-quality
knowledge.

A person should be able to state what they are trying to accomplish in normal language. Lattice
should determine what they actually care about, use appropriate expertise and research to work
through the problem, challenge unreliable or incomplete information, and return an accurate,
verifiable, understandable answer they can act on.

The user should not have to understand AI providers, agents, workflows, research methods, or
the machinery underneath. Solandra sits between the person and the intelligence, preserving
the person's intent and turning that intelligence into useful expert knowledge.

The complexity belongs inside Lattice. The knowledge belongs to the user.
